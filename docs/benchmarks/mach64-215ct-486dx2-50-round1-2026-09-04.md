# Benchmark -- Round 1: pacer-timing (0036) + audio-tier fix, real-hardware validation

Validates two fixes from the same day's investigation
(`docs/PACER-TIMING-INVESTIGATION.md`, `docs/BENCHMARK-PLAN.md`'s
audio-tier-mismatch banner) against real hardware, on the CPU tier where
the original mis-staged-audio datum was recorded
(`docs/benchmarks/mach64-215ct-486dx2-50-2026-09-03.md`,
`13.685015 fps`, `build_sha12=f1f867ccadad`). Owned by the `15fps` peer
session (isolated worktree `/home/claude/git/dossage-dx2-50`, branch
`dx2-50-15fps`); driven on the rig by `vcctrl-c3`; coordinated/recorded
by this session.

## Hardware
CPU: 486DX2, ~50MHz (unchanged from the prior 486DX2-50 leg; physically
  swapped back in from Pentium OverDrive 83 for this round, fresh
  dinspect witness confirmed before staging)
Video: ATI Mach64 (215CT/-ET), 2MB VRAM, `SET
  SDL_HINT_DOS_FORCE_MODE_ID=0x0111` (unchanged requirement, mode-set

## Notes

- **Procedural problem, A1: the title-screen exit key doesn't cleanly
  stop the game on this build/CPU combo.** `waitForKeyOrButton()`
  returns on `SDL_EVENT_KEY_UP` as well as `KEY_DOWN` -- only an in-game
  Escape quits, so Escape at the title screen (or even the *release* of
  the Enter key used to launch `DOSSAGE.EXE`, arriving after SDL installs
  its keyboard handler) starts a new life instead of exiting. A1's first
  attempt produced three lives in one launch before finally exiting.
  **Not discarded**: block 1 (452s, the cold-start life) is a valid
  datum and is the more important finding of the round, not a wasted
  run -- it's what first showed the high-tier number moved from the
  previously-recorded (and now known to be doubly-compromised)
  13.685fps down to ~9.9-10.2fps once correctly staged. Block 2 (428s)
  is a real but different condition (warm-cache second life, 97% world-
  hash-table hit ratio at exit) -- recorded, not treated as an ABBA
  cell. Block 3 (partial, 972 frames) ignored. **Corrected exit
  procedure for all subsequent lives**: press Escape once when the title
  reappears (starts a short life-2), wait ~3s, press Escape again
  (in-game -- clean exit). Worked exactly as predicted on every
  subsequent run, leaving a short (14-16s) trailing RUNMANIFEST block
  each time, well under the round's 60s significance cutoff.
- **A still needed a clean single-life re-run (A2)** because A1's own
  wall-clock bracket spanned three lives plus a 90s recovery detour and
  couldn't be checked against the round's INVALID rule. A2 replicated
  A1's block 1 closely (439s/10.19fps vs. 452s/9.90fps).
- **No DPMI-paging evidence.** `DIR C:\<tree>\CWSDPMI.SWP` and `DIR
  C:\*.SWP /S` returned "File not found" on every life, every tree --
  ruling out the working hypothesis that the 12MB high-tier WAV was
  pushing the DPMI host into paging.
- **Tick loss under high-tier audio -- this round's leading theory,
  REFUTED by Round 2, 2026-09-04.** Round 2's dedicated diagnostic build
  added an in-game CMOS RTC witness (an independent 32.768kHz-crystal
  clock, not derived from the BIOS/PIT tick) and measured `"RTC elapsed
  = 304s (engine time(NULL) = 304s; engine clock gained 0.0%)"` for a
  full real life -- the two clocks agree exactly. **There is no clock
  loss on this rig; strike the working conclusion below.** The
  launch-to-title gaps this note describes are now understood as
  ordinary startup/title-wait variability (the title screen waits for a
  key or joystick event to start the first life, and a floating gameport
  can deliver a spurious button event unpredictably), not measurement
  error -- this round's fps figures (A1/A2, ~9.9-10.2fps) stand as
  measured, not as upper bounds. See
  `docs/benchmarks/mach64-215ct-486dx2-50-round2-2026-09-04.md`. The
  reasoning below is kept for the record, per this project's own
  "correct the record" discipline, not as current advice.
  Launch-to-title wall-clock gaps (load + life, minus reported Game
  time) were small and consistent for low-tier audio (B1: 33s, B2: 60s)
  but large for high-tier (A2: 166s; A1's own gap, before its
  three-life contamination, was on the same order). A direct test ruled
  out WAV load time as the explanation: timed `COPY
  C:\DX50A\MUSIC\SONG.WAV NUL` (12MB) and `COPY C:\DX50B\MUSIC\SONG.WAV
  NUL` (3MB) both completed within a ~7s upper bound (operator's own
  tool-round-trip overhead dominates that bound; true copy time is
  almost certainly a couple of seconds or less for both), with no
  proportional scaling between the 4x size difference -- nowhere near
  enough to explain an 80-166s gap. **Working conclusion: under
  sustained high-tier audio-conversion load, something in the chain
  delays servicing IRQ0 (the BIOS/PIT tick) long enough to lose ticks.**
  This matters because DOS's hundredths-of-a-second clock
  (`gettimeofday()`'s source, and the same tick `uclock()`/
  `SDL_GetTicksNS()` ultimately derive from) is the *only* clock
  available in-game -- both `time(NULL)`-based `Game time` and the
  patch-0036-fixed percentiles read fast **together** when ticks are
  lost, and agree with each other while both are wrong (D's own
  internal check: summed per-stage spans over the full life = 451.9s
  against a reported Game time of 452s -- perfect agreement, and
  irrelevant to the tick-loss question, since both numbers share the
  same compromised clock). **Only the external, human-operated
  wall-clock bracket can catch this** -- which is exactly why the
  round's INVALID rule requires one. Magnitude not yet measured (the
  first-title-paint timestamp needed to isolate it from load time
  could not be captured this round -- ring-buffer capture rotated past
  the window before the byte-jump analysis of a 6000+-line timeline
  dump finished; recorded as an acknowledged gap, not guessed). Net
  effect: **A's true fps is at most the reported 9.9-10.2, real value
  probably nearer 8-9fps -- record the reported numbers as upper
  bounds, not exact figures.**
- **Per-stage cost breakdown (D's `STAGEDBG.LOG`, patch 0037,
  temporary diagnostic instrumentation) locates the cost as
  concentrated, not smeared.** Steady-state per-frame average across
  windows 1-13 (300-frame windows): `render`=32.3ms, `blowup`=11.9ms
  (constant across every window -- fixed per-frame cost, not the
  driver), `present`=2.4ms, `tail`=7.4ms -- 54ms of game work. Against
  that: `pump`=32.4ms (3.23 `SDL_DOSAudioPump` calls per frame at
  ~10ms/chunk -- a 512-frame/23.2ms chunk on this device) plus
  `pacer`=10.3ms, which with essentially every frame over budget is a
  cooperative `SDL_Delay(0)` yield back into the audio thread -- i.e.
  the high-tier audio pipeline runs flat out just to keep up,
  consuming **43% of every ~98.8ms frame**. The low tier does roughly
  40% of that per-chunk conversion work, predicting ~54+12=66ms/frame --
  matching B's measured ~67.6ms almost exactly. This is why the
  campaign's decision rule (device-native audio format, so the
  per-chunk conversion collapses to a `memcpy`, L1c) is the first fix
  queued for Phase 2, with the `blowup` stage's per-pixel SDL call
  overhead (L2) second. Verbatim `STAGEDBG.LOG` in
  `rawlogs/dx2-50-campaign-2026-09/round1/D/D_STAGEDBG.LOG`.
- **CWSDPMI exit-stats: a genuine capture-pipeline capability gap, not
  a timing miss.** The primary VGA-capture pipeline cannot render DOS
  text mode during the exit-stats transition window at all (confirmed:
  the frame the ring buffer records during that window is solid black,
  not a missed-but-present frame); the rig's second camera can see DOS
  text mode but has no ring/history, only live polling snapshots. Would
  need either camera-polling timed exactly on the quit keypress, or the
  game/CWSDPMI writing stats to a file instead of console, to capture
  in a future round.
- **A data-integrity note for anyone reducing this round's numbers
  from chat/relay text rather than the raw files**: an earlier relayed
  transcription of D's `STAGEDBG.LOG` (window w=15's `slack_avg`) read
  `-193.95`, differing from the raw pulled file's actual `-678.71` --
  caught by diffing the raw pull against the hand-reconstructed copy
  before trusting either. The error sat in the short trailing
  (life-2, 157-frame) block, outside every steady-state mean actually
  used above, so it did not affect any conclusion in this record --
  but it's the reason the raw pulled files, not relayed/reconstructed
  text, are the ones treated as authoritative going forward.

## Measurement method

Real hardware via vcctrl (authoritative), Gateway 2000 rig, `gateway2000`
profile. ABBA methodology (A, B, B, A, D) with the round's decision rule
pre-registered before any run: >=0.8fps tier delta means audio is the
dominant lever; <0.3fps means it's off the critical path; between means
both need investigation before Round 2. Fired clearly on B1 alone
(delta ~4.75fps). Per-life wall-clock bracket (launch/keypress to
title-reappear, and life-2's Escape-to-Escape) recorded for every life
per the round's INVALID rule; A1's original bracket was disqualified by
the three-life contamination and is why A2 exists.

Raw files (all cells, both authoritative raw pulls and the
now-superseded reconstructed copies) now live tracked-adjacent at
`rawlogs/dx2-50-campaign-2026-09/round1/{A,B,D}/` (raw:
`<cell>_STDOUT.TXT`/`<cell>_RUNMANI.LOG`/`<cell>_SDLDBG.LOG`, plus
`D_STAGEDBG.LOG`; superseded reconstructed copies moved to each
directory's `reconstructed/` subdirectory) -- the worktree's own
`build/round1/` copies are no longer the reference, per the `15fps`
session, 2026-09-04. Reduction re-run from the raw files by the `15fps`
session confirmed every steady-state number in this record unchanged
from the first (relayed-text) pass.
