Re-confirms Cirrus + 486DX2-66 on the merged fix build (the existing
datum for this pairing, 14.77-14.82fps, predates the merge -- same gap
ViRGE's 486DX2-66 leg had). CPU-only swap from the Cirrus+POD83 leg the
day before -- card untouched, no UVCONFIG concern. **Also the first
result this campaign to NOT reproduce the 15.016779fps/4475-frame
signature** every other pacer-slack-having CPU/card combination has hit
since the merge (486DX2-50/486DX2-66/Am5x86-133/POD83 on ViRGE, POD83 on
Cirrus) -- reproduced twice, so this is a real finding, not scatter. See
Notes for the working explanation. Driven on the rig by `vcctrl-c3`;
coordinated/recorded by this session.

## Results
Average FPS: **14.966555**, reproduced identically across two
  independent lives (4475 frames / 299s, `Game time = 4:59` both times,
  captured verbatim from `FPS.TXT`/`FPS2.TXT`). **PASS** against the
  486DX2-50 KPI line (14.90fps) and in-band against this pairing's own
  pre-merge reference (14.77-14.82fps) -- if anything slightly higher.
  Frame-time samples: "4474 used, 0 rejected any category" both lives --
  clean, not a measurement artifact.

  **Breaks the campaign's established 15.016779fps/4475-frame
  signature -- the first pacer-slack-having CPU/card combination to do
  so, and confirmed reproducible, not scatter.** Every other such
  combination since the merge (486DX2-50/486DX2-66/Am5x86-133/POD83 on
  ViRGE's LFB path, POD83 on Cirrus's banked path -- six prior
  appearances, see `docs/benchmarks/virge-86c375-pod83-2026-09-04.md`
  and `...cirrus-cl-gd5434-pod83-2026-09-04.md`) landed on exactly
  298s/4475 frames/15.016779fps. This pairing lands on 299s/4475
  frames/14.966555fps, both times. Same frame count both regimes --
  what differs is the real wall-clock time those 4475 frames took, by
  almost exactly one second (298.33s ideal vs. this pairing's ~299s
  actual).
1% low: RUNMANIFEST `fps_p50=15.00` both lives; `fps_p95=14.94` (life
  1), `14.92` (life 2) -- tightly clustered with each other and with the
  ViRGE+486DX2-66 leg's `fps_p95=14.94` from the day before. The
  percentile shape (bulk of frames still hit the ceiling exactly) is
  consistent across both LFB and banked at this CPU tier; only the
  average (driven by the handful of frames running just slightly over
  budget) differs enough to tip the rounded life duration into the next
  second.
Simulation rate: still pacer-bound at the 15fps design ceiling -- this
  is not a return to the 486DX2-50 tier's compute-bound regime, just a
  small additional per-frame cost that doesn't come close to threatening
  the KPI.
Audio underruns: not instrumented. `AUDIO_PRESENT` confirmed multiple
  times both lives. Tier-matched build, no pitch/tempo mismatch to
  caveat.
Peak memory: not measured.
Notes:
- **Reproducibility check, not a one-off.** The first life reported
  299s/14.966555fps, unexpected given six prior appearances of the
  298s/15.016779fps figure. Rather than accept or dismiss it on one
  sample, a second independent life was run on the identical pairing
  (no swap, same build, same staged tree) -- it reproduced exactly:
  same 4475 frames, same 299s duration, same 14.966555fps, `fps_p95`
  within 0.02 of the first life. Two independent lives landing on the
  same side of a 298/299 rounding boundary is much less likely under
  pure quantization noise than under a real, small, systematic cost --
  treated as confirmed, not coincidence.
- **Working explanation, not yet root-caused via dedicated
  instrumentation** (flagging the distinction per this project's own
  "don't optimize without numbers" discipline -- this is a
  characterization note, not an action item): `duration_s` is
  integer-second, and the ideal 4475-frame life at exactly 66.667ms/frame
  is 298.33s -- close enough to the 298/299 boundary that a per-frame
  cost as small as ~0.15ms (298.33s -> 299.0s over 4475 frames) is
  enough to tip it over. Cirrus's banked path is already known to cost
  more per-frame than LFB (the whole reason SDL/0019 exists is a real
  aperture defect, and the banked multi-bank walk `SDL/0115` is real
  extra CPU-side work vs. a straight LFB write). At POD83 (much more
  raw CPU throughput than 486DX2-66), that extra cost doesn't push the
  life duration over the boundary; at 486DX2-66, apparently it does,
  consistently. This is consistent with, but not the same claim as,
  the 486DX2-50 tier's much larger banked-path-adjacent cost story
  (Section 1's compute-bound gate) -- this is roughly two orders of
  magnitude smaller and does not threaten the KPI at all, just breaks
  the otherwise-universal frame-timing signature.
- **Corrects an overclaim made earlier in this campaign.** The
  Cirrus+POD83 leg's own writeup, and `docs/BENCHMARK-PLAN.md`'s
  banner/matrix at the time, read the sixth consecutive match to the
  298s/15.016779fps signature as making the figure "conclusively...
  video-path-independent as well as CPU-tier-independent." That was
  wrong, not merely premature -- correcting it here rather than leaving
  it standing, per this project's own discipline (see the correction
  note added to `docs/benchmarks/cirrus-cl-gd5434-pod83-2026-09-04.md`
  and `docs/BENCHMARK-PLAN.md`). The accurate statement: the signature
  holds at every CPU tier tested on LFB, and at the fastest CPU tier
  tested (POD83) on banked, but is not universal across the banked
  path -- 486DX2-66+Cirrus is a confirmed, reproducible exception.
- Visual: matched `docs/screenshots/dossage-gameplay.png` both lives, no
  corruption. Clean two-Escape exit confirmed via the hardware camera
  both times.
- `SDLDBG.LOG` (both lives, identical shape to the POD83 leg):
  `DOSVESA-CTRL: VBE 3.0 total_vram=1024_KB`, `oem_string='Universal
  VESA VBE 6.70'`; `cirrus-lfb-aperture-bug: detected -> forcing banked
  path`; `DOSVESA-MODESET: id=0x01D9 320x240 16bpp has_lfb=1 use_lfb=0
  banked=1`.
- Wall-clock bracket (control-host UTC, life 1): launch 1788624114.74,
  title reappeared 1788624483.13 (delta 368.4s vs. 299s reported life --
  same documented launch/warmup slop pattern as every other leg, not
  clock loss).

Real hardware via vcctrl (authoritative), Gateway 2000 rig,
